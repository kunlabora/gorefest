Design review · whole app

One Gorefest, from sign-in to the table

The new login (layout C) has a clear personality: brand bar, big headline with the action beside it, quiet sunk bands, and real product shown on tilted cards. The rest of the app is still the phone column it started as. Below, every screen redrawn with the same moves. Switch between Desktop and Phone: it is one markup that reflows, not two designs.

What the login teaches the rest of the app

1

One brand bar, everywhere

The wordmark replaces the muted page title. On desktop it gains a nav (Game days, Collection) and your avatar, so there is always somewhere to go.

2

Headline first, action beside it

A kicker, a big Bricolage headline, one line of help, then the primary action in the same eye-line. No hunting for the button in a dock.

3

Two surfaces, no dividers

The page is surface; secondary material sits in a full-width surface-sunk band. Grouping comes from the surface change, not from 24px between everything.

4

Show the product

Real box art on raised cards with the cardboard edge, tilted a degree or two like a hand of cards. Hue tiles and mini screens where there is no art.

5

Desktop is a layout, not a stretched phone

One 1080px container, two columns at 720px and up, and the same markup collapses to the phone column. The 480px strip goes away.

6

The dock is for phones only

Where the action is beside its content on desktop, the sticky dock stays as the thumb-reach version on phone. One brand fill per screen stays the rule.

1 · Home

Your game days, next one first

Today's list treats a game day tomorrow the same as one in December.

  • The next game day is the hero. Big date, title, who is going, the shelf as a fan of covers, and the one thing you still owe it ("Do your taste round").
  • Hosting sits beside it as a quiet secondary card, so the single brand fill belongs to the next step, not to "host".
  • Everything later goes in the sunk band, two columns on desktop. Polls and the BGG nudge live there too.
  • Was: one h1, a column of identical cards, a dock with two buttons.

2 · Game day

The hub reads like the invite it came from

The hub is the screen people live on during the day. It should look like the thing they were invited to.

  • Hero band with date, place and who is coming as three quiet facts, then the title at full size. The "next step" card moves beside it, dark, so it is the one thing that asks for action.
  • The shelf is the sunk band: a six-up grid of covers on desktop, with each owner's avatar on the tile.
  • Players and invite share a row. The copy box sits next to the people it brings in.
  • Was: eight stacked sections of equal weight, the invite link last.

3 · Invite landing

The invite is the login page's twin

Most first-time users arrive here, not at /login. It should pitch and sign in on one screen.

  • Same hero as the login: who invites you, the title at full size, three facts, then a join form with the email inline. Logged-out visitors do not need a second page to start.
  • The shelf is the picture. On desktop, four box covers as a tilted hand, with the count; on phone a small fan under the facts.
  • What happens next in the sunk band, as three numbered steps, so the "optional BGG, then the taste round" path is visible before signing up.
  • Was: a 480px column with the join button in a dock under a long scroll.

5 · Taste round

The question on the left, the cards on the right

  • Phone is unchanged in spirit: question on top, the pair and answers within thumb reach.
  • Was: the same column at any width, so on a laptop the duel is two thumbnails in a strip.

6 · Your favourites

The payoff is the login's hero cards, for real

The login promises "three suggestions" with three tilted cards. The payoff should deliver them in the same form.

  • Copy left, cards right at the same proportions as the login hero. The primary action sits under the headline, the "sharpen it" link next to it.
  • Was: a podium list under a 44px headline, a dock for the actions.

10 · Link BGG

The verification as three illustrated steps

The slowest, most confusing screen in the app: it asks you to leave and edit a profile elsewhere.

  • The code is the headline object. A big dashed copy box with the six characters, copy button attached, instead of a line in a paragraph.
  • The screenshot moves beside the steps (desktop) so you see where to click while you read; each step is a numbered disc, the same as the login's how-it-works.
  • "I've added it" is the one brand fill, with its own row so it does not hide under a dock.
  • Was: a paragraph, a small image, a dock button.

12 · Check your mail

The login's second step, with the mail on screen

The follow-ups to sign-in (code, name, expired link) should be the same hero, not a different, smaller app.

  • Same hero and bar as /login, so the code step feels like the page turned over rather than a new screen.
  • A tilted "mail" card on desktop shows the subject line and where the code is, so people know what to look for in their inbox.
  • The 6-digit input stays huge (36px, spaced); the button sits directly under it. The "send it again" links stay below.
  • Was: a narrow column and a dock button on a laptop. The expired-link, welcome and name screens follow the same pattern; not drawn.

Rolling it out

1

Foundations (done with the login)

Wordmark bar, .gf-screen--wide, hero band, sunk band, sample cards. The login shipped these, so everything below reuses them.

2

Nav and the 480px strip

Add the desktop nav and drop .gf-screen's 480px cap for signed-in pages. Every screen then gets the wide frame, even before it is redesigned.

3

Highest traffic first

Invite landing, taste round, favourites, pick a game. These carry the product's promise; the rest can follow in any order.

4

Docs and tests

Each move updates docs/design (mockups, brand book layout rule) and the web tests' selectors. Not drawn here: expired-link, cancelled and ended pages, the readiness view and collection list, which take the same hero pattern.
