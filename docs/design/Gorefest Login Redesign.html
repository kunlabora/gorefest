1

A phone strip on a laptop

At 720px and up, .gf-screen caps at max-width: 480px, app bar and dock included. On a 1280px screen that's 37% of the width, with no edge or surface change to show where the column ends, so the page looks unfinished rather than focused.

Fix: give desktop its own layout (card, split or two-column hero) instead of a capped phone column.

2

Pitch above the task

Most people on /login are returning users, but the first thing they see is a 17px explainer. The brand is a 15px muted label in the app bar, and the only heading on the page is the email question halfway down.

Fix: lead with brand and the sign-in form; move the explainer below or beside it.

3

Steps read as a list, not "How it works"

An unlabelled <ol> with black discs looks like bullets. Each step is a bold line, a <br> and a muted line. The copy says "teaches it" and "it picks" without saying what "it" is.

Fix: add a "How Gorefest works" heading, give steps their own row or columns, name Gorefest as the subject.

4

One gap for everything

.gf-body puts 24px between every block, so the pitch-to-form gap equals the heading-to-field gap. Nothing groups the explainer as one unit and the form as another. Top padding under the app bar is 8px, everything else 24px.

Fix: tight spacing within a group, a clear break (divider, surface or 48px+) between pitch and form.

5

Asked twice

H1 "What's your email?", then label "Email", with the helper sentence above the field instead of tied to it. The heading and helper sit outside the <form>.

Fix: heading says what the page is ("Sign in to Gorefest"); the label asks for the email.

6

Button cut off from its field

Submit lives in .gf-dock outside <main>, linked by form="email-form". .gf-body has flex: 1, so on any tall screen the button drifts to the bottom with dead space and a border line between it and the input.

Fix: on a one-field form, put the button directly under (or beside) the input.

7

Small stuff

The footer sits below the "sticky" dock, so the dock is never actually at the bottom. A version string on the login screen is noise for users. The tab title is just "Log in", with no product name.

Fix: footer row with BGG credit; version in a muted corner or the settings page; title "Sign in · Gorefest".

Alternative A · Smallest change

Form first

Same components and tokens, reordered. Mostly a template and CSS change.

  • Brand and task on top. A real wordmark, then "Sign in to Gorefest" with the form and its button in one card.
  • Labelled steps. "How Gorefest works" gets a heading and sits below a divider, three columns on desktop.
  • Desktop has a layout. 1040px container; the card is 460px and centred, so the form stays a comfortable width.
  • No dock. The button follows the field; the footer becomes one quiet row.

Alternative B · Two jobs, two panels

Split

The explainer and the form each get their own surface, so they never compete.

  • Desktop splits the screen. Left: a sunk panel with a headline and the steps. Right: the form, vertically centred at 380px.
  • Steps as a path. Numbered brand discs joined by a dashed line, because these really are three steps in order.
  • Phone puts the form first. Sign-in, then the sunk panel as a clearly separate "what is this" block.
  • Spacing does the grouping. The surface change separates pitch from form; no 24px-everywhere rhythm.

Alternative C · Most expressive

Show, don't tell

A small landing page. The steps are shown with mini versions of the real screens instead of described in words.

  • Headline plus inline form. Email and button share one row on desktop, so signing in takes one glance.
  • The product is the picture. Three suggestion cards echo the result screen and use the existing .gf-art--missing hue tiles.
  • Illustrated steps. An A/B duel, an invite link with faces, a three-game shortlist, each in its own band.
  • Cost. Needs a few new components; the hero cards are hidden on phone to keep the form above the fold.

Recommendation

Ship A now, grow into B or C

A fixes every pinned flaw with the existing component set: form first, button attached, labelled steps, a real desktop layout. It's a template reorder plus one @media (min-width: 720px) block.

B is the better desktop page if a lot of first-time visitors arrive through invite links, since the explainer gets equal space without pushing the form down. C is the strongest pitch but needs new illustration components; worth it if /login doubles as the public landing page.
