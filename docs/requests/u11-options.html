A small evening from people's shelves: three ways to do it

The feedback: "I see these games in your library and want to play a, b and c." Below are three different shapes for that, each with its screens to click through, what it costs, and how it answers the open questions. A recommendation is at the end.

Already decided

  • Its own concept next to the game day: not a replacement, not an extension.
  • Same matching rules: player count, time, taste, wishes.
  • The host invites a few friends and sees the games in their linked collections.
  • A friend's "yes" to a game is a heart (U10): a wish, 3 per person at most.

Still open (each option answers them)

  • Does everyone need a linked BGG collection?
  • What do "maybe" and "no" do?
  • What is it called?
  • How are friends invited?
  • How does it relate to the date poll?

The sample group: Anna hosts; Bram, Caro and Lotte have linked BGG; Jonas hasn't. The phones are real app markup (gf-* classes, the app's copy voice), so they follow your device's theme or the sun/moon button. Box art comes from the app's own covers.

A

Library night

Host picks, friends answer

Anna browses everyone's shelves in one list, puts forward a handful of games, and each friend answers yes, maybe or no per game.

Vocabulary: a library night is a small event hosted by one player. Its library is every member's collection put together. The host makes proposals (up to 6 games); friends answer each one. Proposing someone's game asks them to bring it.

Screen flow

Host
  1. Home: Plan a library night
  2. Title, date, place
  3. Copy the invite link to the group chat
  4. The library: propose up to 6 games
  5. Proposals board: who said what
  6. On the night: pick a game
Friend
  1. Invite link: what, when, which games
  2. Join (sign in or sign up; new accounts wait for the admin)
  3. Answer each proposal: yes, maybe, no; bring yours or not
  4. On the night: pick a game

Open questions, answered

Linked BGG for everyone?
No. The library is whoever has linked; Jonas can join and answer, and sees "Link BGG to add your games". If nobody linked, the host can still search BGG like bring-by-search.
Maybe and no?
Both shown to the host on the board. Maybe does nothing in the matcher. No is a small minus for that player when they're at the table (the mirror of the wish bonus, smaller), never a veto.
Name
"Library night" (alternatives: "shelf night", "game night").
Invite
Its own signed link, exactly like a game day's (/l/{token}, HMAC + version, host can rotate). No email invites; admin approval as today.
Date poll
Separate. The date is set when the night is made. Follow-up: a poll's "Pick this date" could offer "Make it a library night".

Reuses

  • Invite links (InviteLinks), joining, account approval
  • Collections and BGG sync; bring-by-search for unlinked people
  • Wishes: MAX_WISHES, the WantedBy / EagerOwner reasons
  • Bring requests' owner answer ("Bring it" / "Not this time") and its opt-out
  • The matcher and the pick-a-game screen unchanged, fed a different shelf
  • Components: gameRow, segmented, copyBox, nextStep, the poll's gf-opt/gf-meter cards

New (roughly)

  • Events: LibraryNightCreated, LibraryNightJoined, GameProposed, ProposalWithdrawn, ProposalAnswered(answer: YES|MAYBE|NO), LibraryNightCancelled, LibraryNightEnded; bringing as owner yes/not-this-time with a librarynight:{id} tag
  • Deciders: propose (cap 6, host only), answer (yes cap 3 via MAX_WISHES), bring
  • Read model LibraryNights: proposals, answers, tally, the night's shelf
  • Matcher: a small "no" minus; wishes = yes answers
  • web.librarynight: create, landing, library, answer, board; home card
  • Privacy: collections visible to the night's members; PersonalData cases; PlayerErased removes them from nights to come

Pros

  • Closest to the feedback: "I want to play a, b and c" is literally the host's proposals
  • The host gets a clear answer per game, including "no", before the night
  • A short list for friends to answer (5 games, not 60)

Cons

  • Biggest build: a second event type with its own hub, invite, join, answers
  • Three answers; "no" in the matcher is new and needs tuning
  • All the work sits with the host; friends can't put a game forward
  • Next to game days and date polls, a third thing to explain on the home page
L

About the size of S17 (date poll) plus U20 (bring requests) together: new event family, four new screens, one matcher change.

B

Wishes first

Friends heart, host settles

Everybody hearts 3 games from everybody's shelves; the most wanted ones that fit the group become the plan, and their owners bring them.

Vocabulary: a game night is the small event. Its pool is every member's collection put together. A heart on a pool game is a wish (U10). The host settles the plan: the games that will be there.

Screen flow

Host
  1. Title, date, place, then the invite link
  2. Hearts their own 3, like everyone
  3. Most wanted: the pool ranked by hearts and fit
  4. Settle the plan (a few games); owners are asked to bring
  5. On the night: pick a game
Friend
  1. Invite link, join
  2. The pool, sorted by "fits all of you" and their taste: heart 3
  3. Owner: "Can you bring Spirit Island?" Bring it / Not this time
  4. On the night: pick a game

Open questions, answered

Linked BGG for everyone?
No, but at least one person must, or the pool is empty. Unlinked people heart other people's games and see "Your games aren't in the pool yet: link BGG".
Maybe and no?
They don't exist. You heart 3 or you don't; not hearting says enough. Nothing new in the matcher.
Name
"Game night" (it reads as the small sibling of a game day).
Invite
Signed link like a game day. No email invites; admin approval as today.
Date poll
Separate; date set up front. The pool could later outlive one night (a standing group), which is the "game-day series" idea from the Later list.

Reuses

  • Wishes almost as-is: SetPlayWishDecider's rules and cap, applied to the pool instead of the shelf
  • Bring requests (U20) for "settle the plan": the host's pick is a request to each owner
  • The matcher twice: to rank the pool ("Most wanted", using its hard filter for player count and time) and on the night
  • Invite links, joining, approval; collections; gf-wish heart chips

New (roughly)

  • Events: GameNightCreated, GameNightJoined, PoolWishChanged, GamePlanned / GameUnplanned, cancelled, ended; bring requests tagged gamenight:{id}
  • Read models GameNightPools (pool, hearts, plan)
  • A pool ranking: hearts × fit, from the matcher's candidates
  • web.gamenight: create, landing, pool, most wanted, owner's bring
  • Privacy: the whole collection is visible to members (bigger exposure than A or C)

Pros

  • Most democratic: friends find the games they love on other shelves
  • No new answer types, no matcher change: hearts already work
  • The host does the least: the ranking does the sorting

Cons

  • Choice overload: 61 games to scroll for 3 hearts (sorting helps, but it's still browsing)
  • Further from the feedback: the host's "a, b and c" is only their own 3 hearts
  • Hearts spread thin in a group of 5: 15 hearts over 61 games can leave no clear winner
  • Shows every member's whole collection to the group
M

A new event family again, but no new answer type or matcher rule; most of the behaviour is wishes plus bring requests with a different tag.

C

Date and games poll

One link, two questions

The date poll learns a second question: "which of these games would you play?" Picking the date makes a game night with the people who can come and the games they said yes to.

Vocabulary: a date poll can ask about games too (a switch on the form). When the host picks, it makes a game night: the small event, with a plan (the games people said yes to) instead of an open shelf.

Screen flow

Host
  1. Home: Find a date
  2. Dates, plus "Ask about games as well": pick games from their shelf
  3. Share the poll link; add friends' games as they join
  4. Results: date cards and game cards; pick the date
  5. The game night is made; on the night, pick a game
Friend
  1. Poll link, join
  2. One page: tick dates, yes or no per game
  3. See the picked date and the plan
  4. On the night: pick a game

Open questions, answered

Linked BGG for everyone?
No. The host starts from their own collection (or BGG search); a friend who links adds their shelf, and the host can add from it while the poll is open.
Maybe and no?
No maybe, to match the dates (yes/no). No is shown to the host only; the matcher ignores it.
Name
No new word for the poll. The result is a "game night".
Invite
The poll's existing link (/p/{token}). Those who can make the date go straight into the game night; no second link.
Date poll
It is the date poll. Without the games switch, nothing changes and a game day is made as today.

Reuses

  • The whole date poll: form, link, join, results cards, "Pick this date"
  • Wishes: a yes becomes a wish when the night is made (MAX_WISHES)
  • Bring requests for games whose owner is coming
  • The matcher and the pick-a-game screen

New (roughly)

  • Events: DatePollGameProposed, DatePollGameAnswered(yes|no); GameNightCreated (from DatePollClosed) with the plan; the night's join/wish/bring events
  • DatePollSummary gains games and tallies
  • Poll form: a games section; results: game cards
  • A small game night page (plan, people, pick a game)

Pros

  • One link, one round of answering: groups usually settle the date first anyway
  • Builds on a shipped feature people already know
  • Fewest new screens

Cons

  • Friends' shelves only show up after they join, so the first proposals come from the host alone
  • Mixes two decisions: someone who can't make it still answers the games
  • A game drops out when its owner can't come on the picked date
  • The date poll gets heavier, and the "game night" it makes still needs its own page
M

Extends one aggregate-free decider family (the poll) and adds a light game night. Cheapest if the game night stays small; grows to L if it needs everything a game day has.

RecommendationOur advice, not a decision

Build A, the library night, in two slices, and borrow one idea each from B and C

A answers the feedback in its own words: the host sees the shelves and says "a, b and c". Friends answer a short list instead of browsing 60 games, and the host knows before the night what will be there.

  1. Slice 1 (M): library night with a fixed date, its own invite link, proposals (6 max), yes / maybe / no. Yes is a heart (3 max). Maybe and no are shown to the host only: no matcher change yet. Proposing someone's game asks them to bring it (U20's owner answer).
  2. Slice 2 (S–M): decide on "no" as a small minus once we've seen real answers; let a date poll's pick make a library night (C's best part).
  3. From B: sort the library by "fits all of you" and by what the host will likely enjoy, using the matcher's filter, so the host isn't scrolling 60 games blind.

Go for B instead if you'd rather the friends drive and the host do little; go for C if the dates are the real pain for small evenings.

Not sure about

  • How much of the game day the new concept copies: checkpoints, play log, closing at midnight, the recap mail. All three options need at least "pick a game" on the night; the rest could come later.
  • Whether the U20 opt-out ("Let people on my game days ask me to bring games") also covers showing your collection to a library night, or needs its own line on join.
  • The caps: 6 proposals, 3 yeses. With 5 people that's 15 hearts over 6 games, which is plenty; with 3 people it may be thin.
  • Whether a library night can grow into a game day later, or stays small for good (5–6 people?).
